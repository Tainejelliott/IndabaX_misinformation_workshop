Before retrieval works, someone has to parse, chunk, and extract structured facts from raw documents. The extraction method you choose shapes everything downstream — what can be retrieved, what can be cited, and what silently breaks.

Most production pipelines combine tiers: cheap rule-based extraction for high-confidence fields, with LLM-prompted extraction reserved for ambiguous cases — always behind a validation step (see Common pitfalls).
Chunking and extraction strip away the surrounding context a reader normally uses to resolve pronouns, word senses, acronyms, entity names, and even register or locale. Each of these has to be resolved before or during ingestion — not left for the LLM to guess at query time.

Date formats are ambiguous without locale context
The shipment contract was signed on 03/04/2024.
If MM/DD/YYYY (US)April 3, 2024
If DD/MM/YYYY (international)March 4, 2024
Only ambiguous when both parts are ≤12 — a date like 25/03/2024 can only be 25 March, since 25 can't be a month. Fix: normalize every date to ISO 8601 (2024-04-03) at ingestion time, and store the source's locale/country as metadata rather than guessing later.
